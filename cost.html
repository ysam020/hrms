Scoped to small and medium companies only — 100 employees is the ceiling for now, matched to what one person can sell to, onboard, and support alone. 02

Where Praesto stands on features

Set against that market, not in the abstract.

Ahead of the market

  • Face match + liveness + GPS attendance built in — a paid hardware add-on at Keka, absent at greytHR/Zoho/HROne
  • AI resume screening on every application automatically — a premium module elsewhere, if it exists at all
  • Passwordless auth (Google + WebAuthn passkeys) — no competitor in this list offers this; all still lead with password + optional 2FA
  • RBAC + ABAC enforced server-side (self/team/all scoping) — most competitors only hide the menu item, not the route
  • Full before/after audit trail across 18 entity types — competitors mostly stop at login history
  • Self-hosted AI assistant, built-in chat, calendar, report builder, webhooks — replaces 3–4 subscriptions a customer would otherwise stack on top (Slack, a BI tool, a chatbot vendor)
  • No setup fee, live in minutes — direct contrast to Keka's ₹25k–1L implementation charge
  • Performance review cycles that actually move money — a manager's recommended increment, once approved through the same approval-chain engine used for leave/resignation, writes a new salary structure automatically. Most competitors treat review and comp revision as two disconnected modules

Behind, or unproven

  • TDS/income-tax withholding is now calculated correctly (see §06 — the bonus-month over-withholding bug is fixed), but Form 16 is provisional/Part B only, marginal relief on the Sec 87A rebate isn't implemented, and surcharge on >₹50L income is hardcoded to zero
  • Zero reference customers, case studies, or reviews
  • One developer, no team — support is email-only with a realistic response time, not a round-the-clock desk. Sets a hard ceiling on how many customers can be onboarded and supported at once

Flagged — not part of the app

Aurora PostgreSQL cluster and DocumentDB cluster (both named database-1) were created the same day this audit ran, both untagged, both console defaults. Nothing in the codebase or either CloudFormation stack references Postgres or DocumentDB. A Lambda function (hrms-scan-upload) has an S3 invoke permission scoped to the praesto bucket, but that bucket has no notification configuration wired up — so it never actually fires. Combined, these are estimated to add ~$150–200/month for infrastructure that does nothing, on top of the $300 total above. Recommend confirming and deleting — this is unrelated to the Atlas decision and still open.

04

Two tiers, and why it stops there

Both tiers ship the same product — full feature set, no module locked behind a higher plan. That's deliberate: differentiator #11 in the sales guide is that Praesto doesn't charge extra for things like biometric attendance the way Keka does, and gating features by plan would quietly undo that pitch. What moves between tiers is price per employee — not functionality, not support quality.

The earlier draft of this memo went up to a Scale and Enterprise tier. Both are dropped here, not deferred to a footnote — with one developer and no team, a 150+ employee company is a support and onboarding commitment that can't be honestly staffed right now. Revisit those tiers once there's someone other than you answering tickets.

Tier Employees Monthly Annual Support
Starter 1–25 ₹4,999 flat ₹3,999 flat Email, 2 business days
Growth 26–100 ₹149/employee ₹119/employee Email, 1 business day
  • Starter charges more per head on purpose. A 10-employee company still needs the same ALB, ASG, and monitoring stack a 90-employee company does — the flat ₹4,999 floor (≈₹200/employee at a full 25 seats, more below that) protects margin on accounts too small to amortize the fixed infra cost on their own.
  • Growth at ₹149/employee is the anchor plan and the number to lead with in conversation — positioned above Keka/HROne's blended rate, justified by attendance security, AI screening, and audit/RBAC depth neither offers at that price. 100 employees × ₹149 = ₹14,900/month, still under Keka's top published tier.
  • Support times are stated, not promised as an SLA. "1 business day" is an honest ceiling for one person also writing the code — better to say that plainly than to imply a support desk that doesn't exist.
  • No setup fee at either tier — deliberate contrast to Keka's ₹25k–1L implementation charge, consistent with the "live in minutes" pitch.
Open decision

Consider a founding-customer rate — e.g. Growth locked at ₹99/employee/month for 12 months — for the first 3–5 paying customers in exchange for a case study or testimonial. Cap it at a small, named number of accounts (see margin math below for why).

05

Margin math

Two cost components: a largely fixed infrastructure floor (EC2/ALB/Amplify/monitoring — doesn't move much with one more employee) and a small variable cost per employee (S3 storage for KYC docs/resumes/chat media, incremental Atlas IO and data transfer, estimated at ≈₹8/employee/month). Because every customer is capped at 100 employees, the ceiling on total revenue is set by how many customers one person can sell to, onboard, and support — not by the infrastructure, which barely needs to grow within this range:

Stage Illustrative mix Employees Revenue/mo Fixed cost/mo Gross margin
First customers 2–3 accounts, Starter + Growth mix ~120 ₹18,000 ₹28,700 –65%
Steady ~7 accounts, mostly Growth ~350 ₹52,500 ₹28,700 ~40%
Solo ceiling ~12 accounts — realistic max for one person ~600 ₹88,800 ₹35,000 ~55%

The first row is a real loss on infrastructure alone, not a rounding error — 2–3 small accounts don't cover the ~₹28,700/month fixed floor. That's expected (the stack is provisioned ahead of revenue) but it's also the strongest argument for keeping the founding-customer discount narrow: giving away Growth at ₹99/employee to more than a handful of accounts stretches the time spent in this loss-making row. By ~12 accounts (~600 employees, the practical limit of what a solo founder can service without hiring), fixed cost has barely moved but revenue has grown 5×, which is the whole point of the cap being on customer count and not on infrastructure.

Not included in this model

This is infrastructure gross margin only. It does not account for Razorpay's transaction fee (~2% + GST on domestic cards, higher for international/UPI edge cases), your own time, or customer acquisition cost. Treat the margin figures above as a ceiling, not a full P&L.

06

TDS & Form 16 — implementation review

Reviewed twice — first against the unstaged working tree, now against the merged, committed state (feat: add tds in payroll, PR #86). New IncomeTaxSlab and TaxDeclaration Prisma models, a tdsCalc.ts calculation engine, a declare→prove→verify workflow mirroring the existing KYC lock/revision pattern, and Form 16 generation via the same jsPDF approach as the payslip. This is a genuinely substantial build — both tax regimes, financial-year-scoped slabs, a YTD/running-total mechanism (which PF/ESI/PT never needed), and 20+ unit tests on the calculation core. The bug that blocked calling this "done" is now fixed (see below); what's left is a smaller, named list of legal-completeness gaps, not a correctness risk.

Fixed — confirmed against current committed code

The bonus-month over-withholding bug is resolved. calcMonthlyTds (tdsCalc.ts:247-249) is now a thin wrapper around computeTdsWorksheet, so the function that actually runs during initiatePayrollRun and the printable worksheet share one implementation — a one-time component (SalaryComponentDefinition.isRecurring: false) is correctly excluded from the rest-of-year projection in both places, so a bonus month no longer inflates withholding, and the worksheet PDF now matches what's actually deducted. This was originally flagged as the blocking issue in this review; it is no longer open.

Correct and solid

Holds up

  • Both regimes modeled correctly, defaulting to New Regime per current law, with old-regime declarations (80C/80D/24(b)/NPS) gated to the regime that actually allows them
  • Slabs are financial-year- and regime-scoped in a proper IncomeTaxSlab table, following the same tenant-override-with-fallback pattern as ProfessionalTaxSlab
  • YTD tax-already-deducted is tracked by summing prior approved/disbursed PayrollLineItem rows within the FY — a real running total, not a guess
  • Declaration→proof→review workflow correctly mirrors the KYC lock/revision pattern, with proper status-transition guards
  • Form 16 Part B is genuinely itemized (exemptions, standard deduction, Chapter VI-A, slab-by-slab tax, month-wise breakup) — not a relabeled payslip
  • 20+ unit tests on the calculation core, with real boundary coverage (87A cliffs, top slab, HRA's 3-leg cap)

Not done yet

  • Section 87A marginal relief isn't implemented — an employee just above the ₹12L/₹5L rebate cliff is overtaxed by several times the legally correct amount
  • Surcharge for income above ₹50L is hardcoded to zero — understates liability for higher earners
  • Form 16 Part A (TAN summary, certificate number, quarterly deposit acknowledgments) is missing — only Part B exists, so it isn't a complete, filable Form 16 yet
  • No admin-side way to generate a Form 16 or TDS worksheet for another employee via the API — a real gap for actual year-end filing, where HR needs to produce every employee's Form 16, not just their own
  • Employer NPS contribution (Sec 80CCD(2), valid under both regimes) isn't modeled
  • No integration tests on the declaration workflow or the payroll/salary/Form 16 wiring — only the pure calculation functions in tdsCalc.ts are tested
  • Done — calcMonthlyTds now excludes non-recurring components from annual projection— confirmed fixed in the current committed code, see the callout above
  • Implement Section 87A marginal relief— employees just above the rebate cliff are currently taxed several times the correct amount
  • Decide the surcharge question explicitly— either implement it, or state clearly (in-app and in the pitch) that income above ₹50L/year isn't supported yet, instead of silently returning zero
  • Scope Form 16 Part A— decide whether it ships before year-end, since Part B alone isn't something a customer's CA can file with
  • Update the pitch to "TDS withholding is correct, Form 16 is provisional"— the withholding bug is fixed, so it's honest to claim TDS is supported; just don't call Form 16 complete until Part A exists and marginal relief/surcharge are handled
